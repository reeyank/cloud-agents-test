Why Mars matters

A planet shaped by fire, ice, and time.

Mars preserves clues about how rocky planets evolve. Ancient river channels suggest that liquid water once moved across its surface, while modern dust storms and polar ice caps reveal an active world still changing today.

Fast facts

  • Olympus Mons is the tallest volcano in the solar system.
  • Valles Marineris stretches farther than the continental U.S.
  • Thin atmosphere means sunsets can glow blue near the Sun.

Surface story

Volcanoes

Massive shield volcanoes hint at a long history of internal heat and ancient lava flows.

Canyons

Gigantic fractures and valleys expose layers of Martian geology like pages in a book.

Water clues

Deltas, minerals, and sedimentary rocks suggest Mars was once much wetter than it is now.

Exploration timeline

Robots are writing Mars history one landing at a time.

1976

Viking 1

Delivered the first successful long-term surface mission on Mars.

1997

Sojourner

Proved that a mobile rover could explore the Martian surface.

2012

Curiosity

Found strong evidence that ancient Mars could once support microbial life.

2021

Perseverance

Collects samples and studies Jezero Crater, a former lake environment.
